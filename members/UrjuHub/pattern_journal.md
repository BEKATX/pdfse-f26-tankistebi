# Pattern journal · Giorgi Urjumelashvili (@UrjuHub) · Team TANKISTEBI

Same prompt every week: Where did you apply this pattern this week, where did you exercise agency beyond your assigned lane, and what did you hack together to test something faster than the plan allowed? Link the DECISIONS.md lines you drove.

---

## Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role
Observed real-world operational friction across campus life, focusing on the basement (-1 floor) laundry facility shared by residents of Blocks F and G. Rather than proposing speculative smart-hardware gadgets, I helped ground the team in the observable physical struggle: students hauling full laundry baskets down multiple flights of stairs only to find every washer running or finished clothes abandoned in drums.

## Week 2 · Evidence over opinion
Applied this pattern by prioritizing verbatim resident testimony over our own assumptions about laundry courtesy. I logged Interview 01 and conducted Interview 03 as the primary asker, specifically investigating how students react when finished clothes are left in machines. This revealed an unexpected courtesy protocol: residents don't mind someone unloading their laundry after 15 minutes, provided it is placed in a clean plastic bag on a clean table rather than dumped on dusty chairs. Stepped outside my lane to help establish our interview consent and logging discipline. Evidence in 01-discovery/interview-logs/03-resident-blockf.md.

## Week 3 · Synthesis over summary
Helped synthesize patterns across our interview logs into clear behavioral tensions rather than raw complaint lists. I logged Interviews 04 and 05, noting how timer drift (machines drifting 10–15 minutes from phone alarms) and un-scanned usage cause students to lose trust in manual tracking. Beyond my assigned lane: I mapped the physical distance friction between upper residential floors and the -1 basement, showing that "out of sight, out of mind" is why clothes sit forgotten in washers for hours. Drove evidence for the Week 3 prediction confirmation in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 4 · Test the riskiest assumption
Tested whether residents would interact with a digital status tracker or default back to the dorm Messenger chat. I conducted Interview 08 with Resident-G203, which proved that resistance to downloading native mobile apps is near 100%, and that students strongly demand a zero-install browser link. Beyond my assigned lane: I worked with BT to define what features must NOT appear in our prototype (no accounts, no payments, no community chat bloat). My hack: I sketched paper card layouts of the 4 machines to test if students understood remaining-time displays before we wrote our prototype prompt. Evidence in 01-discovery/interview-logs/08-resident-blockc.md.

## Week 5 · One metric that matters
Fought against using registered user accounts or app downloads as our metric, arguing that signing up changes nothing if the student still finds full machines. Value is delivered only when a student starts a wash without an empty staircase trip. Beyond my assigned lane: I conducted Usability Session 3 with Resident-G204, where the tester hit a dead end when all machines were busy and tried to fall back to Messenger. This directly drove our decision to add preset cycle buttons (30m, 45m, 60m) and a "Notify when free" button in prototype v2. My hack: I manually seeded test machine records directly in our database so we could test timer calculations before the UI was connected. Drove evidence for line 4 in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 6 · The smallest complete journey
My major contribution this week was defending Step 5 (Unload / Free Machine) in our MVP journey. AK and BT considered ending the journey when the countdown timer reached 0:00, but our discovery logs proved that forgotten wet clothes dumped on dirty surfaces was the #1 friction point between dorm mates. Without an explicit "Unload" action that returns the washer to "Available", the physical problem remains unsolved. Beyond my assigned lane: I audited our Neon Postgres SQL schema and verified .env* was listed in .gitignore to protect credentials. My hack: I used the browser's standard HTML5 Web Audio API to trigger a completion chime rather than waiting for complex native push notification permissions. Evidence in 03-build/mvp-boundary.md and [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 7 · Ship to learn
Watching BT walk our live production link on mobile data during Lab 7 showed how easily analytics break in production. While the database performed well, our client component was triggering duplicate PostHog captures, artificially doubling our North Star metric. Reconciling PostHog against Neon caught this before real dorm students saw the link. Beyond my assigned lane: when setting up the database connection, I found Next.js server components were aggressively caching machine status responses and serving stale countdowns; I added export const dynamic = "force-dynamic" to the API route to ensure live data. My hack: I monitored real-time event streaming in PostHog Activity during the walk to verify latency and property ingestion on cellular networks. Drove evidence for the Week 7 Gate line in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 8 · Read what happened, not what you hoped
Applied this pattern by analyzing why 6 of our 9 field-week wash cycles were left on "Finished" with a 42-minute median idle drum time. Follow-up conversations revealed that students simply don't pull out their phones while carrying wet laundry upstairs. Beyond my assigned lane: I took ownership of our upcoming AI feature for Lab 9, designing the version-zero prompt (03-build/evals/prompt-v0.md) and the 10 starter eval cases (03-build/evals/eval-set.md) for the Laundry Courtesy & Schedule Advisor. My hack: I hardcoded the 15-minute courtesy rule from our discovery logs into the prompt constraints to prevent the AI from ever authorizing students to dump wet clothes prematurely. Drove the Week 8 AI feature line in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).
