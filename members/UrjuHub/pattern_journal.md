# Pattern journal · Giorgi Urjumelashvili (@UrjuHub)

Same prompt every week: Where did you apply this pattern this week, where did you exercise agency beyond your assigned lane, and what did you hack together to test something faster than the plan allowed? Link the DECISIONS.md lines you drove.

## Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role
Instead of waiting for the coding and build phase to start later in the semester, I actively engaged in problem discovery by observing daily friction in student life around campus. I contributed 3 distinct observations to our problem pool (focusing on exam group study coordination, snack availability, and missing cooking supplies in dorms) and helped establish the team contract expectations.

## Week 2 · Evidence over opinion
During the Four Filters evaluation, I pushed the team to choose the Block F laundry bottleneck over my own pooled ideas because the evidence of reachable, daily pain was indisputable. Even though my primary accountability is Build, I exercised agency by logging Interview 01 and conducting Interview 02 with Block F residents. In the interviews, I held back any urge to propose tech features or app ideas and strictly logged verbatim Georgian quotes to capture the real emotional friction (such as wet laundry being dumped on dusty tables). Drove the evidence backing line 1 in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 3 · Synthesis over summary
Analyzed the tension between timer drift and unannounced walk-ins at the -1 floor facility. Stepped outside my build role to cluster quotes around how informal Messenger chat inquiries fail to prevent empty trips. Verified that the real emotional pain point was not waiting time, but clothes dumped on dirty tables.

## Week 4 · Test the riskiest assumption
Applied this pattern by isolating our core technical and behavioral risk: whether residents would check a live status board before leaving their room instead of relying on informal workarounds or assuming timers lie. Beyond my assigned lane: I conducted Interviews 08 and 09 in Block G and Block F to test our clickable mockup concept, which surfaced the critical requirement for a crowd-sourced "Report Incorrect Status / Machine Broken" button and confirmed zero-login browser access was non-negotiable. My hack: I mapped all existing non-software workarounds (plastic bags, floor basket queues, and physical display checks) in `01-discovery/competitive-landscape-seed.md` to benchmark our prototype against reality before writing code. Drove the competitive landscape and root-cause evidence backing the Week 4 line in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 5 · Build to fail, measure value
Built Prototype v1 using v0.dev strictly following our 3-screen plan for the -1 floor laundry flow and deleted tool-generated auth/settings pages. Authored the analytics event schema in `03-build/analytics/event-schema.md`, defining the `resident_starts_wash` north star event and setting up a reconcile plan to catch double-fires on unstable dorm Wi-Fi. Beyond my assigned lane: I pushed back against using registered accounts as a metric and ran Usability Session 3 with Resident-G204, uncovering the dead-end when all 4 machines are busy. My hack: I manually seeded the 8 machines in our local database before any frontend UI was connected so we could verify state transitions immediately. Links: `README.md`, `03-build/analytics/event-schema.md`, `docs/ai-usage-log.md`.

## Week 6 · The smallest complete journey
Applied this pattern by defending Step 5 (Unload / Free Machine) inside our MVP boundary in `03-build/mvp-boundary.md` while cutting user accounts, push bots, and advance queue reservations. Even though stopping at 0:00 would have been simpler to code, our logs proved that un-cleared machines cause wet clothes to be dumped on dusty chairs—so without an explicit unload state, the core journey would remain incomplete. Beyond my assigned lane: I worked with BT to trim Sprint 1 scope down to an honest 20-hour budget and verified that zero PII (no names or room numbers) was stored in our telemetry schema. My hack: I implemented the 0:00 cycle alert using standard HTML5 Web Audio API beeps instead of waiting on complex browser push notification permissions, and added `export const dynamic = "force-dynamic"` to prevent Next.js from caching stale countdowns. Drove the Week 6 MVP boundary line in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 7 · Ship to learn
Shipped the machine unload flow and status reset API (`S4`) and reviewed the building selector tabs (`S2`) on the live Vercel deployment before our Lab 7 phone walk. Watching BT walk the production URL on mobile data confirmed our Neon Postgres writes worked cleanly, while our PostHog reconciliation immediately caught a duplicate client `capture()` call that was double-counting starts—proving why live telemetry verification beats local assumptions. Beyond my assigned lane: I monitored the live PostHog ingestion stream during the lab walk to verify `ms_latency` and cycle duration properties arrived intact, and took responsibility for onboarding Resident-G204 in Block G for field week. My hack: I tested our audio alert and timer completion transitions locally by manipulating session timestamps in Postgres rather than waiting 45 minutes per real wash cycle. Contributed to the Week 7 gate line in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).

## Week 8 · Read what happened, not what you hoped
Applied this pattern during our field-week readout in `03-build/analytics/break-readout.md` by confronting our biggest funnel drop: 9 real wash cycles started, but 6 were left un-cleared on the board with a median idle drum time of 42 minutes. Instead of assuming residents ignored the board, follow-up interviews showed students leave their phones upstairs while carrying wet laundry—so I scoped and grounded our AI Laundry Courtesy & Schedule Advisor (`03-build/evals/prompt-v0.md` and `eval-set.md`) directly in live machine states and our 15-minute courtesy threshold to reduce idle drum time without causing hallway disputes. Beyond my assigned lane: I took ownership of follow-up outreach with Resident-G204 to investigate how waiting students react when a machine sits in "Finished - Needs Unloading". My hack: I drafted a 10-case evaluation set using real field-week scenarios and verbatim Georgian queries before writing any LLM integration code so we could test courtesy rule compliance first. Drove the Week 8 AI feature line in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md).
